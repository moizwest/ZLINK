Epoxy flooring for warehouses in the UAE: what to check before you choose

A plain-English guide for facility managers and warehouse owners in Dubai, Sharjah and Abu Dhabi, from the team that lays these floors.

Start with the slab

An epoxy floor is only as good as the concrete under it. New concrete needs to cure before it is coated, typically around 28 days unless the system specification says otherwise. Moisture rising through the slab is the most common cause of blistering and peeling, so the slab should be checked for moisture before any coating is chosen. Cracks, damaged joints and loose areas are repaired first.

Preparation decides the result

The surface is mechanically prepared, usually by diamond grinding or shot blasting, to remove the weak top layer (laitance), old coatings and contamination and to give the primer something to grip. A floor that is only cleaned and painted will not last under warehouse traffic, whatever product is used.

Match the system to the traffic

Thin roller-applied coatings suit light foot traffic and dust-proofing. Self-levelling and thicker build systems suit forklifts, pallet trucks and heavy wear, and give a smoother, flatter finish. Ramps, loading areas and wet zones need an anti-slip finish, usually made by broadcasting aggregate into the coating. Hard-wheeled forklift traffic calls for a thicker build than foot traffic.

Outdoors and in sunlight

Standard epoxy changes colour (yellows) and loses gloss under direct sunlight. For open car parks, entrances and outdoor areas, a UV-stable topcoat such as polyurethane is normally specified over the epoxy, or a system designed for outdoor exposure is used.

UAE heat and application

Temperature and humidity change how long the material stays workable and how fast it cures. In summer, work is often planned for cooler hours, and the manufacturer's limits for substrate and air temperature are followed. Cure times before foot traffic and vehicle traffic come from the product data sheet, so ask your contractor for both before you plan the shutdown.

Finishing details

Coving (a curved joint between floor and wall) makes food and clean areas easier to wash. Line marking for walkways, bays and forklift routes is usually done in the same programme. Expansion joints are kept and sealed, not coated over.

Working in a live warehouse

Most warehouses cannot close. The floor can be done in phases, area by area, with barricades and signage, and with night or weekend shifts where needed. Agree the phase plan before work starts so stock and racking moves are ready.

What to ask a contractor

Ask for a site survey before the price, an itemised quotation naming the system and its thickness, the preparation method, the phasing plan and cure times, and the warranty terms in writing. A price without a named system and preparation method cannot be compared fairly with another.
